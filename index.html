Features

What you're actually getting

Every set shares the same base: Cherry profile, an MX-style cross stem and PBT plastic. Tap through for the details.

Cherry profile

Each row sits at a slightly different height and angle, so the board curves toward your fingers. It's lower than the OEM caps most keyboards ship with and far lower than SA, which most people find easier on long typing sessions.

MX-compatible cross stem

The cross-shaped stem fits any switch with an MX-style "+" top: Cherry MX, Gateron, Kailh Box, Akko, Outemu and most hot-swap boards. Low-profile and Topre switches use different stems and won't fit.

PBT, not ABS

PBT is denser than the ABS plastic used for most stock keycaps. It keeps its matte texture instead of turning shiny where your fingers rest, and it doesn't yellow. Topo and Shadow print their legends on the front edge so backlighting shows through; Script uses dye-sublimation, where the ink sits inside the plastic.

A lower, rounder sound

Thicker, denser PBT damps the high "clack" of thin ABS and pushes the sound lower. How much depends on your switches, case and plate, so treat any keycap as one part of the sound, not all of it.

Will these fit my keyboard?

If your switches have a "+" shaped stem (Cherry MX, Gateron, Kailh, Akko and most hot-swap boards), yes. Shadow (137 keys) and Script (142) cover full-size boards with spares. Topo comes in 97 keys for compact boards or 133 for full size.

What does shine-through mean?

On Topo and Shadow the letters are printed on the front edge of each key in see-through material, so your keyboard's backlight glows through them. Switch on Backlight in the configurator to see it.

How do I swap keycaps?

Pull each key straight up with a keycap puller, then press the new one straight down until it sits flush. A full board takes about 15 minutes.

Is the 3D model exactly what I'll get?

It's a close preview of the shape, colour and legend style. Use the product photos for exact print details, and check the colour you pick in good light.
